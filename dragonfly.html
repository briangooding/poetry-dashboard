Dragonfly notebook · Dec 2018 to Mar 2022

How the poems changed

A poem a day for three and a quarter years. Read in order, the notebook moves through seven stretches: from long, argumentative winter essays in verse to short, plain notes written at home, with a loud midwinter relapse in 2021 in between.

    What never changed

    The migraine behind one eye

    Named in every year, often with the exact side: "slightly behind and above my left eye" (Jan 2019), "explosion behind my right eye returns" (Jun 2020), "migraine, right eye" (Mar 2022).

    Staring out windows

    Second-story windows, north-facing windows, the east window. The window is the standing vantage point, and "out the window" shows up in every stretch.

    The self-instruction

    "keep moving forward", "try not to", "I'm trying". Present from 2019, it becomes the dominant voice in 2021 and 2022.

    An unnamed "her"

    Hair, voice, a glance across a coffee shop. The muse is rarely named, and when a name does appear it is often struck through or renounced.

    Elsewhere

    Taipei, Hangzhou, Bangkok, Rotterdam, Bremen, Berlin. Memories of other countries surface in every stretch as the counterweight to parking lots and strip malls.

    Animals and deep time

    Savanna, jungle, australopithecus, egrets, rattlesnakes. The body is repeatedly read as an old animal in the wrong habitat.

    How this was made

    • Source: the poems table in the poetry database, collection [2018-2019] - dragonfly notebook, last synced . Only numbered poems count. The numbers come from the database function analysis.page_data.
    • Every poem was read in date order. The stretches and their descriptions come from that reading; the numbers support it, and every excerpt is checked against the database text each time the page is refreshed.
    • Dates come from the written field. Where it was empty or more than five days off the created date ( poems, for example 07.08.09 for 2019 or 04.22.22 for 2021), the created date was used.
    • Poem text is everything above the footer rule (a line of two or more dashes). Lines exclude blank spacer lines; stanzas are counted from blank-line breaks. "Tagged" means the poem's tags list is not empty.
    • Theme shares come from case-insensitive whole-word matching on the poem text, using the word lists in analysis.themes.
    • Many poems have been re-tagged in a later revisit, mostly adding place tags such as Texas. That is why the last quarter shows as almost fully tagged.
